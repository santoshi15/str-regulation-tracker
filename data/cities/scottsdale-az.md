# Scottsdale, AZ: Short Term Rental Rules

Last verified: 2026-10-25
Summary for tracker research only. This file is general information and is not legal advice. Arizona state law limits city regulation of vacation rentals by classification or use, and HOA rules can add restrictions.

## Permit or license required and type
- Scottsdale license required for each property under Scottsdale Ordinance 4566. The city owners page states rental owners and operators must obtain a Scottsdale license for each property and comply with safety, health, and neighborhood notification requirements.
- A Transaction Privilege Tax license from the Arizona Department of Revenue is required for each rental property, and the TPT number is needed to apply for the city license.
- Each property must also be registered with Maricopa County under ARS 33-1902 before occupancy. The city states it is illegal to rent a property before it is registered with the Maricopa County Assessor.
- Vacation and short term rentals are dwelling units rented for less than 30 days to transient guests, allowed by right in all residential districts subject to city conditions, because ARS 9-500.39 limits city bans based on classification or use.

## Registration steps and fees
- Steps in city order: Step 1, obtain a TPT license from the Arizona Department of Revenue for each property. Step 2, apply for a Scottsdale license for each property. Step 3, within 30 days of receiving the city license or when contact information changes, notify all single family properties adjacent to, directly across from, and diagonally across from the property, or all units on the same floor in a multi family building, with a letter showing the license number, the property address, and the emergency contact name, address, and 24 hour phone, and complete the neighbor notification and liability insurance attestation. Step 4, register with Maricopa County. Step 5, generate and display the required short term rental notice inside the front door and the primary backyard door area as described in Section 18-175(h).
- Insurance: liability coverage of at least $500,000 for every short term and vacation rental property, held directly or through an online lodging marketplace.
- City license fee: $250.00 license fee listed on the city Business and Regulatory Licenses page for vacation and short term rentals.
- Penalty listed on that same city licenses page: $1,000 penalty for every 30 day period the applicant fails to apply for the license.
- Sources: City of Scottsdale, Information for Owners and Operators, https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/codes-and-ordinances/vacation-and-short-term-rentals/information-for-owners-and-operators , verified 2026-10-25; City of Scottsdale, Business and Regulatory Licenses, https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/licenses , verified 2026-10-25

## Occupancy limits
- Maximum family size: 6 adults and their related dependent children for all dwelling units, including vacation and short term rentals.
- All dwelling units and any accessory guest houses must be rented together and may not be rented independently.
- Non residential uses including retail, restaurant, banquet space, and event center uses are prohibited.
- Source: City of Scottsdale, Information about Vacation and Short Term Rentals, https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/codes-and-ordinances/vacation-and-short-term-rentals/information-about-vacation-short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25; City of Scottsdale, Business and Regulatory Licenses, https://www.scottsdaleaz.gov/licenses , verified 2026-10-25

## Lodging and transient tax rate and who collects
- Taxes are collected by the State of Arizona. The city states all vacation and short term rentals must pay Transaction Privilege Sales Tax and a Transient Tax, commonly called a bed tax, and both are collected by the State of Arizona through the Arizona Department of Revenue TPT license.
- City Transaction Privilege Tax rate stated by the city: 1.75 percent. That figure is the Scottsdale portion described on the city page.
- Transient Tax rate stated by the city: 5 percent imposed on properties used as a vacation or short term rental. The city page describes how that revenue is split for destination marketing and tourism uses.
