## Cities where rules are in flux

- New Orleans, LA: highest flux. A commercial interim zoning district expired November 5, 2025, program terms have shifted between Residential and Commercial and NSTR and CSTR labels, and allocation runs on a quarterly lottery calendar that extends into January 2027.
- Austin, TX: staged change across February 2025, October 2025, and July 1, 2026 for platform duties. License structure, site limits, and filing duties all changed inside the last two years.
- San Diego, CA: license fees changed March 1, 2025, TOT zone rates changed May 1, 2025, and Tier 3 availability is shrinking toward its 1 percent cap while Tier 4 sits closed.
- Palm Springs, CA: Chapter 5.25 carries amendments into 2026, a co owned housing program started in 2024, and a city workgroup continues to review density and housing supply questions.
- Nashville, TN: a new online application process took effect March 11, 2026 and the STRP pages carry March 2026 update dates.
- Savannah, GA: the registration portal change in 2024 and the hotel motel tax move from 6 percent to 8 percent leave legacy forms on the city site.
- Sioux Falls, SD: the permit program itself started January 1, 2024 with fees from July 1, 2024, and the city page flags a card service fee starting January 5, 2026.
- Scottsdale, AZ, Denver, CO, and Asheville, NC: no pending change found on the primary pages reviewed on 2026-10-25. That finding is a snapshot of those pages, and each file says so.
