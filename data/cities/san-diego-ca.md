# San Diego, CA: Short Term Residential Occupancy Rules

Last verified: 2026-10-25
Jurisdiction: City of San Diego.
Summary for tracker research only. This file is general information and is not legal advice. The city STRO page itself carries the same caution and directs readers to the full ordinance text.

## Permit or license required and type
- A Short Term Residential Occupancy license, called an STRO license, is required for all STRO of a dwelling unit, or part of a unit, for less than one month within the City of San Diego. It has been unlawful to operate an STRO without a license on or after May 1, 2023.
- Four tiers: Tier 1 Part Time, Tier 2 Home Sharing, Tier 3 Whole Home excluding Mission Beach, and Tier 4 Mission Beach Whole Home.
- A host may hold only one license at a time and may not operate more than one dwelling unit for STRO at a time. Licenses are not transferable between ownership or location.
- Prerequisites before applying: an active Transient Occupancy Tax Certificate, an active and paid Rental Unit Business Tax account, and, when the host is not the owner, a Business Tax Certificate plus a right to occupy document such as a lease allowing STRO or a signed owner statement or the city owner authorization form.
- Source: City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25, page updated October 23, 2026 on the page retrieved

## Permit cap and waitlist status
- Tier 3 cap: licenses will not exceed 1 percent of San Diego total housing units outside the Mission Beach Community Planning Area, based on the most recent SANDAG demographic and socioeconomic housing estimates, last updated March 2024 on the city page.
- Tier 4 cap: licenses will not exceed 30 percent of the Mission Beach Community Planning Area on the same SANDAG basis.
- Tier 1 and Tier 2: unlimited in the city stats table.
- Live counts on the city page as of October 23, 2026: Tier 1 issued 135 with unlimited remaining, Tier 2 issued 2,335 with unlimited remaining, Tier 3 issued 4,854 with 807 remaining, Tier 4 issued 1,099 with 0 remaining, grand total 8,423.
- Application status on that page: the application period for Tier 1, 2, and 3 is open, and the application period for Tier 4 is closed. The Tier 4 waitlist application period is also closed on that page. A separate city page lists a Tier 4 Applicant Waitlist document, so waitlist existence is confirmed and current openness is closed per the main STRO page.
- Lottery history: Tier 3 and Tier 4 licenses were allocated through a lottery methodology adopted by Council, with good actor weighting for Tier 4 applicants who had paid TOT, had recent booking activity, and had fewer than three verifiable complaints, per the city lottery project page.
- Sources: City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25; City of San Diego, STRO Lottery project page, https://www.SanDiego.gov/panda/projects/stro-lottery , verified 2026-10-25; City of San Diego, STRO Tier 4 Applicant Waitlist, https://www.sandiego.gov/document/office-city-treasurer/stro-tier-4-applicant-waitlist , verified 2026-10-25

## Registration steps and fees
- Fees effective March 1, 2025 per Council approval on the city page. Application fee plus license fee: Tier 1 $33 plus $193, Tier 2 $33 plus $284, Tier 3 $41 plus $1,129, Tier 4 $41 plus $1,129.
- Renewal fees effective March 1, 2025 are the same amounts: Tier 1 $33 plus $193, Tier 2 $33 plus $284, Tier 3 $41 plus $1,129, Tier 4 $41 plus $1,129.
- All fees are non refundable. Licenses expire two years from issuance and may be renewed under Section 510.0106(d). Renewal notices are emailed 60 days before expiration.
- Tier 3 and Tier 4 utilization rule: the ordinance requires STRO utilization for a minimum of 90 days each year during the license term to maintain a Tier 3 or Tier 4 license under Section 510.0107(c).
- Quarterly reports are required only for Tier 3 and Tier 4 hosts.
- Source: City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25

## Owner occupancy rule
- Tier 1 Part Time: rented for an aggregate of 20 days or less per year, and the host does not need to reside onsite during the STRO.
- Tier 2 Home Sharing: renting a room or rooms in the home for more than 20 days per year so long as the host resides onsite, and the host may be absent from the permanent residence during a whole home STRO for up to 90 days per calendar year.
- Tier 3 and Tier 4 Whole Home: rentals for more than 20 days per year where the host does not reside onsite.
- Source: City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25

## Minimum stay rule
- Tier 3 and Tier 4: a guest two night minimum stay is required, per the tier table on the city page.
- Tier 1 and Tier 2 minimum stay: no minimum is stated in the tier table retrieved. Status for those tiers: UNVERIFIED from the city page reviewed on 2026-10-25.
- Definition threshold: occupancy for less than one month is STRO, with a month defined as the period from the first calendar day of occupancy to the same calendar day in the next month, or the last day of the next month when no corresponding day exists.
- Source: City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25

## Lodging and transient tax rate and who collects
- Transient Occupancy Tax rates effective May 1, 2025, per the City Treasurer TOT page: Tax Zone 1 11.75 percent, Tax Zone 2 12.75 percent, Tax Zone 3 13.75 percent. The prior rate through April 30, 2025 was 10.50 percent.
- Tourism Marketing District assessment: 2 percent, levied on lodging businesses with 70 or more rooms, so a typical single home STRO falls outside that room count threshold as described on that page.
- Collection and remittance: the Municipal Code requires operators to collect TOT from transients, hold TOT in trust, and remit TOT to the City Treasurer monthly, and TOT must be collected at the same time as rent under Section 35.0112. A Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate is required for a property rented to transients for less than one month.
- Platform duty under the STRO ordinance summary on the STRO page: hosting platforms must collect and remit TOT to the city when the platform collects rent from guests, must refuse transactions for a unit without a valid STRO license, and must provide monthly data and keep records for four years.
- Rental Unit Business Tax also applies to property owners who rent all or part of a property for more than six days in a calendar year, per the city pages.
- Sources: City of San Diego, Transient Occupancy Tax and Tourism Marketing District, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/tot , verified 2026-10-25; City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25

## Enforcement and penalties summary
- A November 21, 2022 city release states enforcement for unlicensed or improperly licensed STRO properties began May 1, 2023 and that measures may include Administrative Citation Warnings, Administrative Citations of up to $1,000, and Civil Penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, with unpaid penalties or continuing violations possibly resulting in a property lien with the County of San Diego.
- The current STRO page states hosts who fail to comply risk enforcement up to and including civil penalties and license revocation, and it routes enforcement questions to the Building and Land Use Enforcement team.
- Sources: City of San Diego, November 21, 2022 STRO application deadline release, http://sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11-21_stro.pdf , verified 2026-10-25; City of San Diego, Short Term Residential Occupancy, https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy , verified 2026-10-25
