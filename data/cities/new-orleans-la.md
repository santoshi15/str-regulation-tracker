# New Orleans, LA: Short Term Rental Rules

Last verified: 2026-10-25
Jurisdiction: City of New Orleans and Orleans Parish.
Summary for tracker research only. This file is general information and is not legal advice.
Rules in flux flag: high. See the recent changes section.

## Permit or license required and type
- STR permits and licenses are required and are administered by the Short Term Rental Administration in the Department of Safety and Permits.
- Two tracks appear in current city materials: NSTR for Non Commercial Short Term Rental and CSTR for Commercial Short Term Rental. City CZO definitions and council materials also use Residential and Commercial classifications in the ordinance history. Confirm the current track name and eligibility in the One Stop App and with the STR Administration before filing, because the city program pages and the CZO update cycle use overlapping terms.
- Owner permit and operator permit are separate. The operator is the person who operates or manages the unit, and the CZO NSTR definition requires an operator who physically resides in a bedroom on the lot during guest stays in the NSTR setting described below.
- Licenses are non transferable, per city materials for CSTR and per council materials for residential and commercial permits.

## Permit cap and waitlist status
- NSTR block cap in the CZO definition: only one lot within each city block, inclusive of lots fronting any exterior boundary of the block and interior lots, may be permitted for use as a Non Commercial Short Term Rental. Only one NSTR license is allowed per lot under that definition.
- City lottery announcement language adds nuance: a September 2024 city announcement states to check the NSTR License Map to see if a square is open, with 1 NSTR License and 2 Special Exception NSTR Licenses allowed per square. A square in New Orleans usage can span a block area, so a tracker entry must check the live map for a specific square.
- CSTR cap in the CZO use standards excerpt: no more than one unit or 25 percent of total units on a lot or a single building across lot lines, whichever is greater, may be permitted as a Commercial Short Term Rental, with exceptions for the VCE district, Canal Street frontage between the Mississippi River and Rampart Street, and EC, MC, LS, and MI districts as listed in that excerpt, plus an added affordable housing condition in CBD-5.
- Allocation method: lottery. The STR Administration publishes lottery calendars with quarterly application periods and lottery dates, for example the 2026 periods and lotteries listed on the city lottery calendars page.

## Registration steps and fees
- NSTR lottery steps summarized from city announcements: attend required training before the application period as a new owner or operator applicant, close open violations or open permits or seek building official approval to proceed with an open permit, confirm zoning and addressing, have all owners sign attestation where there are multiple owners, move property out of an LLC name into the owner personal name with the Assessor if it is registered under an LLC, obtain a local picture ID listing the property address for an operator license, and check the NSTR License Map for square availability.
- CSTR steps on the city home page: apply for a Non Structural Renovation permit on the One Stop App with the description CSTR License so zoning can review and refer to CPC, wait for conditional use approval, then submit picture ID, floorplan, evacuation plan, owner attestation, and noise abatement, sanitation, and security operation plans. A noise monitoring device has been required for all CSTRs effective July 1, 2023.
- Fees from the City Council ordinance summary for the program: STR Owner Permit per dwelling unit: Residential Partial $250, Residential Small $500, Residential Large $500, Commercial $1,000. STR Operator Permit: $0 when the owner is the operator, $150 for operators of 1 dwelling unit, $1,000 for operators of 2 or more dwelling units. STR Platform Permit: $10,000. Nightly Occupancy Fee: Residential $5 per night in the excerpt retrieved, with the commercial nightly figure cut off in that excerpt.
- Current fee verification caution: the council page retrieved is an August 2019 program announcement. I checked the current STR Administration home page on 2026-10-25 and found current lottery calendars and CSTR submission steps, with no fee schedule published in the text retrieved there. Treat the fees above as ordinance summary fees from the council source and reverify the amount in the One Stop App before budgeting. ## Owner occupancy rule
- NSTR: the CZO definition states the operator, who may not be a guest, physically resides in a bedroom on the lot of record containing the STR during any guest stays and, if required by City Code, maintains a primary residence on the lot. The operator and all owners must be natural persons age 18 or over in that definition.
- Council program summary states the residential permit carries a homestead exemption requirement, ensuring owners reside at the property full time, and it describes whole home STR rentals in residential areas as barred under that residential track.
- CSTR: follows commercial zoning and conditional use, with security, sanitation, and nuisance plans required per council and city materials.
- District bans in the council summary: STRs are banned in the French Quarter except for Bourbon Street and in the Garden District under that program announcement. Confirm current district status in the CZO, because an interim zoning district affecting commercial STRs expired on November 5, 2025 per the CZO text retrieved.

## Occupancy limits
- NSTR under the CZO definition: each permit shall provide no more than three guest bedrooms and six total guests for overnight paid occupancy.
