# Nashville, TN: Short Term Rental Rules

Last verified: 2026-10-25
Jurisdiction: Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County.
Summary for tracker research only. This file is general information and is not legal advice.

## Permit or license required and type
- A Short Term Rental Property permit, called STRP, from Metro Codes is required before listing. The city page states the law requires anyone wishing to rent a property on STRP websites to receive a permit from the Metro Codes Department prior to listing, under Metro Code Section 6.28.030.
- Two permit types: Owner Occupied and Not Owner Occupied. Each type covers a dwelling with no more than four sleeping rooms, rented to a single party.
- A separate Business Tax License may also be required based on annual gross income, per the Davidson County Clerk page. That page lists no license below $3,000, a Minimal Activity license for $3,000 to $99,999, and a Business Tax License at $100,000 and above, with fees shown as $15 or $30 depending on district.
- Sources: Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25; Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property Permit Types, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals/permit-types , verified 2026-10-25; Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property Business License, https://WWW.NASHVILLE.GOV/departments/county-clerk/business-services/short-term-rental-property-business-license , verified 2026-10-25

They do not state a citywide percentage cap or a waitlist on the pages reviewed.

## Registration steps and fees
- Owner Occupied checklist items shown on the city site: proof of payment of all taxes due including property taxes, and for renewals all applicable room, occupancy, and sales taxes; a floor plan of the entire dwelling on letter size paper showing rooms, walls, doors, windows, and smoke detectors for each floor, certified for single and two family dwellings by a state licensed architect, engineer, or home inspector; proof of homeowner fire, hazard, and liability insurance with liability limits of at least $1 million per occurrence; a notarized affidavit; and proof of notification to adjacent property owners.

## Owner occupancy rule
- Owner Occupied permit: the owner must permanently reside at the property and must be a natural person or persons. LLCs, corporations, trusts, partnerships, joint ventures, and other entities are ineligible for owner occupied permits. Proof requires four documents giving proof of owner occupation, and the primary residence address must match the deed recorded with the Register of Deeds. Only one permit per lot in single family and two family zoning districts.
- Not Owner Occupied permit: available only in the zoning districts listed in the cap section above.
- Source: Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property Permit Types, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals/permit-types , verified 2026-10-25

## Minimum stay rule
- Minimum: the owner shall not receive compensation to permit occupancy for a period of less than 24 hours.
- Maximum stay for any guest: 30 consecutive days under the STRP definition on the main city page.
- Sources: Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25; Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property Operation Rules and Requirements, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals/operation-rules-and-requirements , verified 2026-10-25

## Occupancy limits
- Maximum occupants at any one time: no more than twice the number of permitted sleeping rooms plus four, with a maximum of 12.
- Additional operating limits: simultaneous rental to more than one party under separate contracts is prohibited, the principal renter must be at least 21 years of age, and up to four sleeping rooms are covered under the permit types.
- Source: Metro Nashville, Short Term Rental Property Operation Rules and Requirements, https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/short-term-rentals/operation-rules-and-requirements , verified 2026-10-25

## Enforcement and penalties summary
- A permit may be revoked by a court or by the Department of Codes Administration upon determination of three violations of any ordinance or law of general application, or if the Zoning Administrator determines the application contained false or misleading information.
- A permit cannot be transferred or assigned to another individual, person, entity, or address, and a change in ownership results in cancellation of a permit, including a change from a person to a trust or LLC or the reverse.
- The local responsible party name and telephone number must be posted inside the unit, and the responsible party must answer calls 24 hours a day, 7 days a week during each rental period.
- Dollar fine for operating without a permit: UNVERIFIED in the Metro pages reviewed on 2026-10-25.
