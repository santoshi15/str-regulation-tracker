# Austin, TX: Short Term Rental Rules

Last verified: 2026-10-25
Summary for tracker research only. This file is general information and is not legal advice.

## Permit or license required and type
- An STR operating license issued by Austin Development Services Code Compliance is required. The city defines an STR as a residence rented for less than 30 consecutive days and states STRs are regulated by city ordinance and licensed by the city.
- Properties in Austin full purpose jurisdiction and limited purpose jurisdiction require an operating license. Properties in the extraterritorial jurisdiction do not need a license and do not pay local Hotel Occupancy Tax, per the city page.
- Licenses are non transferable and do not convey with a sale. The operator must include the license number in any advertisement, and a person may not advertise an unlicensed unit.
- Sources: City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25; City of Austin, Operating Licensing, https://www.austintexas.gov/page/operating-licensing , verified 2026-10-25

## Permit cap and waitlist status
- No citywide numeric cap or lottery is stated on the city STR page reviewed. Availability is controlled by per operator and per site limits adopted in 2025 and effective October 2025:
- Single family sites: an individual may operate up to two STR units on a site, and may operate additional STRs elsewhere provided they are at least 1,000 feet apart.
- Multi family residential sites: an individual may operate the greater of one unit or 10 percent of units.
- Mixed use sites with four or more residential units and at least one commercial use: an individual may operate the greater of one unit or 25 percent of units.
- Waitlist: UNVERIFIED. I checked the city STR page on 2026-10-25 and found processing queues of 6 to 8 weeks for single family and 8 to 10 weeks for multi family, with no waitlist program stated.
- Source: City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25

## Registration steps and fees
- New license fee: $736.60 total, comprised of a $642.00 license fee plus a $94.60 notification fee. Fees are non refundable.
- Renewal fee: $369.60 total, comprised of a $275.00 renewal fee plus a $94.60 notification fee. Licenses are valid for two years. Renewals may be submitted 60 days before expiration.
- Steps: create an Austin Finance Online account for a property in full purpose jurisdiction, gather a copy of the front of the owner government ID, proof of tenancy if applicable, and a notarized Agent Authorization Form if the applicant is not the owner, submit online, in person, or by mail, email the ID copy as instructed, and complete Hotel Occupancy Tax account setup before a license is issued.
- October 2025 changes listed by the city: a Certificate of Occupancy and proof of insurance are no longer required for new applicants or renewals, and tenants may operate STRs with landlord permission.
- Source: City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25

## Owner occupancy rule
- No primary residence or owner occupancy requirement is stated in the October 2025 rules summarized on the city page. Tenants may operate with landlord permission. A local contact must live in the Austin Metro Area, covering Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, and Caldwell counties, and must respond to emergencies within two hours.
- Status note: I checked the city STR page on 2026-10-25 for an owner occupancy rule and found the tenant permission rule and the local contact rule, with no owner occupancy condition listed.

## Lodging and transient tax rate and who collects
- City of Austin Hotel Occupancy Tax: 11 percent, comprised of 9 percent occupancy tax plus 2 percent venue project tax.
- State of Texas Hotel Occupancy Tax: 6 percent, remitted separately to the state. The city page presents the city tax as in addition to the state rate.
- Collection and filing: every person owning, operating, managing, controlling, or collecting payment for occupancy must collect the city tax from guests. Platforms including Airbnb, Vrbo, Expedia, and Booking.com are required to collect and remit Hotel Occupancy Tax on behalf of owners for platform bookings as of April 1, 2025 for city purposes and July 1, 2026 for platform regulation duties described on the STR page. Owners must still file a quarterly report showing how much each platform collected and remitted, must file a zero report when there were no rentals, and must collect and remit directly for non platform revenue.
- STRs in limited purpose jurisdiction are exempt from city Hotel Occupancy Tax and still require a license.
- Sources: City of Austin, Hotel Occupancy Taxes, https://www.austintexas.gov/financial-services/hotel-occupancy-taxes , verified 2026-10-25; City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25

## Enforcement and penalties summary
- The city directs violation reports to Austin 311 online, by phone during business hours, or through the Austin 311 app, with Code Compliance handling enforcement.
- Advertising or operating without a license is listed as a guest and operator violation area on the city page. Specific dollar penalties: UNVERIFIED in the city STR page reviewed on 2026-10-25. That page states platforms must remove unlicensed listings upon city request under the platform rules effective July 1, 2026, and it states licenses can be denied with appeal notice by mail.
- Source: City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25

## Recent or pending changes
- Rules in flux: yes, recent and staged.
- February 2025: City Council adopted changes making STRs an accessory use to all residential uses in all zoning districts when the unit holds a valid operating license.
- October 2025: two year license term, tenant operators allowed, per site limits, local contact geography, neighbor notification at every renewal, and removal of the Certificate of Occupancy and insurance proof requirements took effect.
- July 1, 2026: platform regulations took effect, requiring a license display field and removal of unlicensed listings on city request.
- Source: City of Austin, Short Term Rentals, https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/short-term-rentals , verified 2026-10-25
